Terrestrial planets are rocky, solid planets. They come in three or more different layers, from the core, the mantle, and the crust. The planets are made out of silicate rocks, metals, or silicate minerals. Terrestrial planets have very few moons, even no moons for some of them. Some examples of terrestrial planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars.

>Gas Giants are planets that are mostly made out of hydrogen and helium, instead of solid rock. All gas giants have rings, not as big as Saturn’s rings, but still quite large. They also have many moons, unlike terrestrial planets which have a lot less moons. They are a lot larger than terrestrial planets as well, and a lot more mass than them. Two close examples of this are Jupiter, and Saturn.

Ice giants are like gas giants, just as big and large, but they use heavier elements than hydrogen and helium. They usually have water, methane, and ammonia. Ice giants also have a lot of moons, just like the gas giants. Some examples of ice giants are Neptune, and Uranus.
