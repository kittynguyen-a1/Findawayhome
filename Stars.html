Stars

Stars also come in three different types, main sequence stars, giants and supergiants, and stellar remnants. Stars are all made of hot plasma held together by their own gravity, which shapes it into a sphere or spheroid. Unlike a planet, stars emit heat and light, which helps planets like Earth thrive and have life.

Main sequence stars are stars that actively fuse hydrogen into helium. It is also the star’s most stable period in its life, because the outward pressure generated by the core balances the gravity compressing the star. It is also the longest period of the star's life. Some stars in this phase are; The Sun, Sirius A, and Alpha Centauri A. Colours can vary, but the most common are blue, bluish white, yellow, orange, and red. Blue stars are hotter than red because they emit shorter wavelengths, which means higher frequency wavelengths, and red emit longer wavelengths, which means lower frequency wavelengths.

Giants and Supergiant stars are massive, luminous stars that are thousands of times bigger than the sun. This occurs when a star runs out of core hydrogen, and then they expand significantly. They are also brighter than the sun, and have a mass around eight to 100 times bigger than the sun. Some common colours for both are red, yellow, orange, and blue.

Stellar remnants are the remains of dead stars. They are dense, compact core materials left behind when they exhaust their natural fuel. Different types of stars will have different stellar remnants, but the most common ones are white dwarfs, neutron stars, and black holes. The Sun will most likely turn into a white dwarf when it dies.
